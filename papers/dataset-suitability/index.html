Dataset Suitability for Predictive Maintenance in Conveyor-Based Factories: A Technical Review and Evaluation Framework

Hoàng Phụng · NexaEvo, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam

Abstract

Predictive maintenance for conveyor-based factories is often framed as a model-selection problem, although the more fundamental difficulty is whether available data support the intended prediction claim. Public datasets span current-fault classification, accelerated bearing degradation, synthetic maintenance records, and industrial vibration monitoring; these tasks are not interchangeable. This technical review develops a practical framework for selecting public data and evaluating an initial conveyor-maintenance study without overstating evidence. It combines a targeted review of conveyor and bearing sources with reproducible inspections of two downloadable datasets. A conveyor spreadsheet hosted on Figshare contains 1,500 records and five balanced operating-state labels but no timestamp, asset identifier, or future-event endpoint. The UCI AI4I 2020 dataset contains 10,000 synthetic records and 339 machine-failure labels; an always-negative rule therefore achieves 96.61% accuracy while obtaining zero failure recall. These examples show why temporal provenance, asset-level separation, lead time, event recall, and false-alarm burden matter more than headline accuracy. For an initial public-data study, complete bearing run-to-failure datasets such as XJTU-SY are suitable component-level prognostic candidates, while conveyor-specific sources such as SSCC better support present-state detection. Neither establishes factory-wide predictive performance. The paper concludes with an evaluation protocol and a minimum data specification for a future AI-and-IoT conveyor pilot.
